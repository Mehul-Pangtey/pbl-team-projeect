        Virtual City Simulator

        A Graph-Based City Management and Traffic Simulation System

        About the project

        A small smart city modelled as a graph. Intersections are vertices and roads are weighted edges (weight = road length in metres). Buildings sit at intersections, vehicles travel along shortest routes, and a one-second simulation clock drives traffic signals, road capacity and utility consumption. This website is a browser version of the original C++ console program and follows the same rules.

        Graphs and adjacency lists

        adjacencyList[i] holds every road leaving intersection i as { to, length }. Adding a road stores it in both directions, so roads are bidirectional. An adjacency list only stores real connections, so Dijkstra visits only actual neighbours.

        Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm

        Uses a distance array (all ∞, source = 0), a parent array, and a custom binary-heap min-priority queue of (distance, intersection). The smallest entry is removed, outdated entries are skipped, and each neighbour is relaxed if dist[u] + length < dist[v]. The path is rebuilt by following parent[] back from the destination and reversing it. Edge weight is road length only.

        Queues and priority queues

        Every simulation second, active ambulances go into a priority queue and are processed first, lower vehicle number first. All other active vehicles go into a normal FIFO queue and are processed afterwards in creation order. Dijkstra uses a separate min-priority queue.

        Traffic signal simulation

        A 70-second cycle based on time % 70. Roads leading to even-numbered intersections are green for seconds 0–29, yellow 30–34 and red 35–69. Roads leading to odd-numbered intersections are red 0–34, green 35–64 and yellow 65–69. Only green allows a vehicle to enter a road.

        Road capacity

        Each road direction holds at most 3 vehicles. A vehicle increments the counter when it enters a road and decrements it when it reaches the next intersection. If the road is full, or the signal is not green, the vehicle waits at the intersection without taking a slot.

        Vehicle management

        Vehicles are Car (10 m/s), Ambulance (15 m/s) and DeliveryBike (6 m/s), each with a precomputed Dijkstra route. Statuses are PARKED, MOVING, WAITING and COMPLETED. Distance travelled is added up from the actual movement in each second.

        Emergency and food delivery routing

        An ambulance must start at an intersection with a hospital. A delivery bike must start at a restaurant and end at a house. Both use the same Dijkstra route calculation and the same simulation engine as every other vehicle.

        Resource management

        Water demand = population × 150 + hospitals × 500 + restaurants × 300 litres/day. Electricity demand = population × 2 + hospitals × 50 + restaurants × 30 kWh/day. Each simulation advance adds daily demand × seconds / 86400 to consumption.

        Budget allocation

        The city starts with ₹5,00,000. An allocation needs a department and a positive amount no larger than the remaining budget. A successful allocation lowers the remaining budget, raises total spending and is added to the history.

        Technology

        HTML5, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript (ES6) only. No frameworks, libraries or server. The map is SVG drawn by JavaScript. The city is saved in the browser's localStorage, so a refresh keeps your progress.

        What this project does not do

        No artificial intelligence, machine learning, GPS tracking or real-world traffic data. Dijkstra finds the shortest route by length, not the fastest or least congested one.
